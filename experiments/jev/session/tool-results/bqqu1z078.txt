## 3. Reading the output: probabilities, confidence, thresholds

**`confidence` is a deterministic transform of the probability distribution, not a learned estimate of correctness.** [Confidence page](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence.md)

- Choice: `confidence = (p_max - 1/n) / (1 - 1/n)` where n = number of options. Only the top probability counts.
- Score: 1 minus the probability-weighted mean distance from the most likely level, normalized by an even spread, floored at 0.
- Noul: no confidence field. Docs suggest `|2p - 1|` for uniform gating; starting thresholds 0.7 for "yes" and 0.3 for "no".

**Rules that follow from measured behavior:**

1. **Tune thresholds on your own labeled data.** The 0.5 default is wrong per task: median F1-optimal Noul threshold was 0.35 across 31 benchmarks; tuning on just 10 labeled items lifted F1 from 0.71 to 0.79. [Guo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29429v1) · [Deußer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37647)
2. **Do not carry a threshold from a Noul to a Choice (or between frameworks).** A Noul and a yes/no Choice about the same thing return different numbers. Pydantic AI's "confidence" is a margin from the threshold; TanStack omits it on booleans. [awesome-jev-use-cases](https://github.com/walidboulanouar/awesome-jev-use-cases) · [Pydantic AI](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/models/decision/)
3. **High confidence is reliable; mid-range is not.** On Banking77, confidence ≥ 0.99 covered 58% of inputs at 96.3% accuracy; < 0.50 was 29.6% accurate. In 15,346 production coding-agent judgments, the model was wrong 15% of the time below 0.8 and under 1% above. [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/blog/insights/jev-vs-claude-opus-5-classification/) · [pi-warden](https://github.com/DevMortimer/pi-warden)
4. **Calibration is good pooled, bad per task.** Pooled ECE 0.047 but median per-benchmark ECE 0.168; per-dataset ECE ranges 0.003 (sentiment, MMLU) to 0.28 (emotion, legal clauses). Audit calibration on *your* distribution. [Guo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29429v1)
5. **Choice hides an "I don't know" mass.** Noul is the best-calibrated primitive (soft accuracy 0.918), Score next (0.886), Choice worst (0.764). Always add an escape option to every Choice. [Porcedda, Sys1Cal-v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35342)
6. **Per-option probabilities are not coherent.** P(X) + P(not X) misses 1 by ~0.06; three exclusive labels can sum to 1.14. Renormalize in code if you decompose. [Li, He, Li](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33209)
7. **Use three bands.** Act automatically / proceed cautiously / route to a human or an LLM. TypeSafe's worked examples use ≥ 0.9 for high-stakes auto-actions and a 0.5 floor for human routing. [System One concept page](https://docs.typesafe.ai/concepts/system-one.md)

```python
from typesafe_sdk import Choice, Noul, Score, TypeSafeClient  # reads TYPESAFE_API_KEY

with TypeSafeClient(model="jev-1.14.0") as client:
    response = client.system_one(
        state=ticket_text,
        questions={
            "department": Choice(
                instructions="Which team should own this ticket?",
                criteria={"billing": "Charges, refunds, invoices",
                          "technical": "Bugs, outages, integrations",
                          "sales": "Pricing, upgrades, new purchases",
                          "other": "Does not fit the above"},
            ),
            "is_urgent": Noul(instructions="Does this ticket need a reply today?"),
            "frustration": Score(
                instructions="How frustrated is the customer?",
                criteria=["Calm", "Annoyed but civil", "Angry or threatening to leave"],
```

## 5. Writing good questions (the prompting rules for Jev)

Jev reads questions **literally** and reads **option names** more than option definitions. Write accordingly. Sources: [Jaggedness page](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13.md), [Sun et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26758), [Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38827v2)

1. **One judgment per question.** Do not hide several judgments in one. Split an interpretive question into two or more literal ones and combine in code.
2. **State exact conditions and boundary cases** in `instructions`. "Needs a reply today" beats "urgent."
3. **Name options with the words a careful human would use for that category.** When name and definition disagree, the name wins (AUC fell from 93.8% to 23.2% when mappings were swapped). Do not use `true:` to mean "no." Avoid negations inside criteria.
4. **Add an escape option to every Choice**: `other`, `none_of_these`, `not_stated`, `not_enough_information`. Without it the model assigns up to 96% to one option on empty input. [medRxiv evaluation](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.09.26365153v1)
5. **Prefer several Nouls over one Score for detection.** One severity Score caught 4 of 7 critical log events; four Nouls caught 7 of 7. Score compresses toward the middle on ordinal scales (37.7% macro accuracy ordinal vs 84.4% nominal). Use Score as a soft signal and threshold it; never interpolate magnitudes from it. [nexibeo/jev-cookbook](https://github.com/nexibeo/jev-cookbook)
6. **Prefer per-option Nouls over one high-cardinality Choice when precision matters.** At a 0.90 threshold, option-wise Nouls retained 97.0% accuracy vs Choice's 79.4%. [Du et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18529v1)
7. **Dates, numbers, counts: compute in code, put the result in the state.** If you must extract a date, use a Choice over enumerated parts with a "not stated" option and do the math yourself. [Date extraction cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/date_extraction_cookbook.md)
8. **Keep the state minimal.** Strip irrelevant content first (in code, or with a relevance Noul). Batch *questions*, not *items*: many questions per call is nearly free; many items per state costs accuracy.
9. **Reorder options and compare** when stakes are high. The argmax is fairly stable (1.8% flips on reversal) but probabilities shift by up to 34 points; in a chess harness 39-43% of picks changed with option order. Averaging over rotations is a known fix. [Imperatrice et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19619) · [AnyJev](https://github.com/nokia-applied-research/AnyJev)
10. **Jev cannot generate, so get candidates elsewhere.** Extract candidates with regex or an LLM and let Jev select (invoice extraction went 40/40 this way). "Chaining Choices" to synthesize output is slow and weak.
11. **English works best.** Test other languages on your own content. 1.14's largest claimed gain is multilingual [vendor].
12. **Pin the model version** and **cache decisions** keyed on (model, state, questions); reruns are then exact and free.

## 6. Patterns that work (with measured results)

| Pattern | What it is | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Code owns thresholds and side effects** | Jev returns probabilities; your code holds the policy, tuned per consequence ("a wrong FAQ route and a wrong payment route should not share a threshold") | Every shipped guard/router; [Wavect review](https://wavect.io/blog/jev-ai-decision-model-review/) |
| **Confidence-gated cascade** | Jev answers the easy majority; below a threshold, hand off to an LLM or a human | Banking77: cascade at 0.90 handled 75.9% on Jev, 84.0% overall (Opus alone 84.4%) at ~28% of Opus cost. [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/blog/insights/jev-vs-claude-opus-5-classification/). Caveat: flash-tier LLM judges repeat >85% of Jev's confident errors, so cascade to a reasoning model or a human for independent signal. [Rao & Callison-Burch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29769) |
| **LLM for text, Jev for decisions** | Generator produces text or candidates; Jev picks or scores; Jev never writes | jev-ultrafast (browser agent: Jev picks CLICK/TYPE/SCROLL + target, LLM only for typed text) [browser-use/jev-ultrafast](https://github.com/browser-use/jev-ultrafast); typesafe-computer-use at ~$0.0002/decision [awlevin](https://github.com/awlevin/typesafe-computer-use) |
| **Predict-then-decide (two calls)** | Ask Jev to predict the missing fact, write the prediction into the state, then ask the decision | Game-theory decisions 33% → 94%; ALFWorld 31% → 87% with code-supplied lookahead. [Yang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01834v2) |
| **Batch many questions per call** | 1-64 questions per request at flat latency | 13 questions in one call: 12.2x cheaper, 10x faster, same answers. [Parallel questions cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/parallel_questions) · [jgrep](https://github.com/keltokhy/jgrep) |
| **Option-wise Nouls, renormalized** | One Noul per option, combine in code | Phishing detection 62.6% → 95.0% by decomposing into five questions plus logistic regression [greennode](https://greennode.ai/blog/what-is-jev) |
| **Compute facts, then ask judgment** | Put computed values (dates, totals, diffs) into the state | Chess blunders 35% → 5-9% with "assisted facts"; dedupe errors 1 → 0 with code-checked phone numbers [nexibeo](https://github.com/nexibeo/jev-cookbook) |
| **Shadow mode first** | Run Jev alongside the existing decision, log disagreements, then switch | pi-jev, stuntdouble, Wavect all recommend it |
| **Fail-open vs fail-closed, chosen by consequence** | Guards hold or ask on low confidence or API failure; routers fall back to the default model | [Pydantic AI](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/models/decision/) (hand-offs go to LLM, outages fail loudly); [jev-router](https://github.com/fatwang2/awesome-jev) ("Jev failure never blocks the CLI") |
| **Log inputs, link verdicts to quotes** | Jev gives no reasons; the audit trail is the input | Required by every guard author; [Willison's black-box objection](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/21/jev/) |

**Reference implementations worth reading:** [nexibeo/jev-cookbook](https://github.com/nexibeo/jev-cookbook) (15 tested recipes: triage, tagging, dedupe, PII, reranking, extraction), [keltokhy/jgrep](https://github.com/keltokhy/jgrep) (semantic line filter, ~$13 per million lines), [DevMortimer/pi-warden](https://github.com/DevMortimer/pi-warden) (coding-agent tool-call guard, 18k replayed calls), [realZachi/pg-jev](https://github.com/realZachi/pg-jev) (Postgres `jev(row, condition, threshold)` predicate), [TypeSafe cookbooks](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/parallel_questions), [TypeSafe patterns](https://docs.typesafe.ai/patterns/fan-out).

## 7. Known failure modes (so you can route around them)

TypeSafe's own list ([Jev 1.13 jaggedness](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13.md)), each confirmed in the field:

| Failure | Measured effect | Workaround |
|---|---|---|
| Literal reading | Answers the question as written, not as meant | Spell out conditions; split questions |
| Counting / arithmetic / numeric encodings | MathQA 17.7 pts below every LLM tested; packet-feature classification 28% vs random forest 70% [Li X.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11978) · [2610.00376](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00376) | Compute in code |
| Dates and times | Treated as text; ordering/durations unreliable | Enumerate parts, compute in code |
| Indirection, double negatives, multi-hop | Two-step word problems 32% correct [Hume](https://archerhume.com/posts/jevs-architecture-unmasked/) | Predict-then-decide; precompute |
| Irrelevant state | Accuracy drops with unrelated content | Filter first |
| Adversarial content | Routing 100% → 0-6% under injection; one appended "opinion" flips 12.1% of decisions [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04985) · [JevAdvBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31142) | Sanitize; deterministic validators; separate injection-detection Noul |
| Contradictory criteria | `true` meaning "no" degrades accuracy | Align names with meaning |
| Option order | First-option bias (vendor) / end-of-list bias (chess test); probabilities shift up to 34 pts | Rotate and average; escape option |
| No generation | Chained Choices "slow and weak" | LLM or regex for candidates |
| Choice miscalibration | Hides P(unknown); confident nonsense on empty input | Escape option; per-option Nouls |
| Ordinal compression | Score bunches in the middle | Nouls per level; threshold only |
| Abstention | -11 to -12 pts when the right answer is "none of the above"; picks "I don't know" when it should answer 10.5% of the time [Du et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18529v1) · [Madrid-García](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34024v1) | Escape option prevents confident nonsense; it does not raise accuracy |
| Non-determinism and drift | ~12% chosen-option change on repeat; `jev-latest` changes | Pin version; cache |
| Rubric judging | Weakest category (GoEmotions macro-F1 0.24; legal clause F1 0.20) [Deußer et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37647v1) | Decompose into literal checks |
| Vision | Preview only; "all models perform worse on multimodal input"; Box always returns a box | Pair Box with a presence Noul; prefer DOM/OCR/accessibility text where available |

## 8. Security rules (non-negotiable for agents)

1. **Treat `state` as untrusted, argued input.** There is no privileged instruction channel; text inside the state can steer the answer. [JevAdvBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31142)
2. **Jev is never the only gate** for destructive or financial actions. Keep a deterministic validator over typed-parsed fields that the model cannot override; one such rule reached 100% where typed-model guardrails failed open 93-100% under a misleading option label. [Azizi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12292)
3. **Confidence is not authorization.** Attacked decisions were as confident as the originals, so confidence-based escalation does not catch attacks.
4. **Ask for injection detection explicitly if you need it.** Jev detects injection well when asked (40/40 in one screen; 0.99 on a planted RAG injection) and is steered by it when asked something else. [OpenRouter tutorial](https://openrouter.ai/blog/tutorials/jev-vs-llm-when-to-use-each/) · [RAG cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/classifying_rag_passages)
5. **Do not put secrets or sensitive attributes in the state.** Outputs leak: hidden attributes (gender, age, health, ethnicity) were inferred with 100% accuracy from labels alone; an Oct 24 incident exposed customers' noul outputs. [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04985v1) · [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/typesafe-ai-admits-a-leak-exposed-every-customers-jev-model-output/)
6. **Guards should fail closed or ask; routers may fail open.** Decide by consequence and expose the choice as a parameter.
7. **For EU residency or on-prem**, Jev is US-hosted with ZDR only via enterprise sales; use an open model behind the same wire protocol (Section 9).

## 9. Drop-in alternatives (same wire protocol)

Code written against `/v1/systemone` with `noul/choice/score` runs against these by changing the base URL (and sometimes the model name). Jev still leads on OpenRouter volume (~1.36B requests/week) and on [Artificial Analysis'](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-decision-index) knowledge-weighted index.

**Licensing trap:** several leaderboard leaders are non-commercial (H2O-Lightning-31B, Jeff-1.x, StartLux weights, Torchcast weights, Bespoke Nimble v3). Check the license before shipping. **Size floor:** ~4B parameters for Jev-level general quality; 0.6-2B models and encoders (Laya, GLiNER2.5-Decide) score near zero on chance-corrected indices unless fine-tuned in-domain. **Serving caveat:** Ollama's raw softmax discards author calibration; quantization below Q8 costs 1-4 points.

## 10. Pre-ship checklist

- [ ] Every Choice has an escape option.
- [ ] Thresholds tuned on ≥ 10 (ideally hundreds of) labeled examples from *your* traffic, per question, per consequence.
- [ ] Nothing numeric, temporal, or counted is left for the model to compute.
- [ ] State is filtered to relevant content and under 32k tokens per question (64k per request).
- [ ] Model version pinned; decisions cached; inputs logged with each verdict.
- [ ] Behavior on API failure, 429/529, and low confidence is explicit (fail-closed for guards, fail-open for routers) and tested.
- [ ] Shadow run completed; disagreement rate with the previous decision mechanism measured.
- [ ] Order-sensitivity checked on a sample (rotate options, compare).
- [ ] Security: deterministic validator in front of any destructive action; untrusted text sanitized or screened with an injection Noul; no secrets in state.
- [ ] Base URL and model name are configuration, so the backend can be swapped for an open model.
